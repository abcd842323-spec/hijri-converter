About the Umm al-Qura calendar

What is Umm al-Qura?

Umm al-Qura (“Mother of Cities”, a name for Makkah) is the lunar Hijri calendar used officially in Saudi Arabia for civil and religious dates. Month lengths are calculated in advance from astronomical rules based on the moon’s conjunction and moonset in Makkah — it is not decided month-by-month by sighting alone.

Why can dates differ by a day?

A Hijri year has 354 or 355 days, so Hijri dates move about 11 days earlier each Gregorian year. Because other countries use local moon-sighting, the same day can be, for example, 29 Ramadan in one country and 1 Shawwal (Eid) in another. That ±1 day gap is normal.

How this converter works

It uses the Umm al-Qura calendar data built into modern browsers (Intl islamic-umalqura). Gregorian → Hijri is read directly; Hijri → Gregorian is found by searching that same calendar, so both directions agree with each other. No date is sent to a server.

Useful to know

1 Muharram 1 AH corresponds to 19 July 622 CE (proleptic Gregorian). Hijri years are written “AH” (After Hijrah). This converter covers roughly 622 CE to 2500 CE / 1 AH to about 1930 AH, subject to browser calendar data.
